# Business case, ROI and mutual action plans

## When to build one

Any deal where the buyer must justify the spend to someone else. If your champion has to present
internally without a business case, you are letting an amateur pitch your value in a room you're
not in.

## ROI model structure

1. **Baseline** — the current cost, using the client's own numbers. Never your estimates. If they
   won't give numbers, the pain isn't real enough yet — go back to discovery.
2. **Cost of inaction** — that baseline projected over 12–24 months
3. **Expected change** — conservative, mid, aggressive scenarios. Lead with conservative.
4. **Investment** — total cost including implementation, training, and internal time
5. **Payback period** — in months. This is the number executives actually remember.
6. **Assumptions** — listed explicitly. A model with hidden assumptions gets destroyed in review.

Use conservative numbers deliberately. A business case that survives a skeptical CFO is worth more
than one that looks impressive and gets picked apart.

## One-page executive summary

For the economic buyer, one page:
Problem in their words → cost today → proposed change → investment → payback → risk and mitigation →
decision needed and by when.

No product description. They're approving a business outcome, not buying software.

## Mutual action plan (MAP)

A shared, dated plan from now to go-live, with an owner on each side for every step. Built *with*
the client, not sent to them.

| Step | Owner | Date | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Technical validation | Client IT | | |
| Business case review | Economic buyer | | |
| Security review | Client security | | |
| Legal and procurement | Both | | |
| Signature | Economic buyer | | |
| Kickoff | Both | | |

Two benefits: it surfaces hidden approval steps early, and a client who refuses to build one is
telling you the deal isn't real.

## Enterprise lifecycle stages

1. Account mapping and prospecting
2. Discovery and pain identification
3. Technical validation and executive alignment
4. Business case and ROI
5. Procurement, legal, security
6. Close, onboarding, value realization

Value realization is a sales stage, not a support stage. The measured result is what makes the
renewal, the expansion, and the referral possible.

## Pre-proposal checklist

Do not send a proposal until:

1. The economic buyer has confirmed a budget source
2. The ROI model uses buyer-provided numbers
3. Legal, security and procurement steps are mapped with rough durations
4. A mutual action plan with dates exists
5. You know who else is being evaluated, including "do nothing"
6. Your champion has told you what could kill this internally

Missing three or more of these means the proposal is a hope, not a sales step. Say so.
