# Prospecting and lead generation

Feeding the top of the funnel. The command `/find-clients` maps here. Match the motion to the
deal type first — outbound for enterprise, territory work for SMB — the tactics are different.

## Define the ICP before searching

A list is worthless without a target. Nail these first:

- **Segment** — industry, company size, geography, revenue band.
- **Trigger** — the event that makes them need you *now*: funding, new hire in a relevant role,
  expansion, a regulation, contract expiry, a public complaint, seasonality. No trigger → cold list.
- **Buying role** — who feels the pain, who signs, who can veto. Prospect the pain-owner, not the
  gatekeeper.
- **Disqualifiers** — who looks like a fit but never buys. Naming these saves more time than any
  outreach tactic.

State the ICP as one sentence: "[role] at [segment] experiencing [trigger], where [disqualifier]
is absent." If you can't fill the trigger slot, the outreach will be generic and get deleted.

## Where to find them

| Channel | Best for | What to pull |
|---|---|---|
| LinkedIn | B2B, mid-market, enterprise | Role changes, hiring signals, posts revealing pain |
| Google Maps / 2GIS | Local SMB, field sales | Category + district lists, reviews (pain signals), hours |
| Industry catalogs / registries | Logistics, manufacturing, trade | Exporters, importers, producers by HS code / direction |
| Company websites / job boards | Any | Hiring for a role adjacent to your product = active need |
| Instagram / Facebook | Local, consumer-facing SMB | Activity level, current tooling, follower complaints |
| Telegram / niche communities | Regional, fast-moving markets | Warm intros, group activity, buying intent posts |
| Existing clients | Everyone (cheapest lead there is) | Referrals, lookalikes on the same street / in the same vertical |

Referrals and lookalikes from happy clients convert several times better than any cold source.
Exhaust them before buying cold data.

## Qualify the list before touching it

Don't outreach a raw list. Score each entry: does the trigger apply, is the pain-owner identifiable,
is there a disqualifier? A short qualified list beats a long cold one — reps burn credibility
blasting generic messages to people who were never a fit.

## The first touch

- **Lead with them, not you.** First line is a specific, verifiable detail about their business.
  Generic openers ("Надеюсь, у вас всё хорошо") get deleted.
- **One result, one number, one question.** Not a pitch — a reason to reply. See `SCRIPTS.md`
  (cold call) and `PROPOSALS.md` (cold email) for exact wording.
- **Match channel to segment.** Field visit for the café; LinkedIn + email for the enterprise
  evaluator; Telegram where the market actually lives.
- **Multi-touch, multi-channel.** One message is not outreach. A sequence — email, call, social —
  over two weeks, each carrying something new, is.

## Response-time is the highest-leverage metric

An inbound lead contacted in 5 minutes converts far better than one contacted in an hour. Before
optimizing any script, check follow-up speed — it's usually the cheapest win available. See `KPI.md`.

## Output for a `/find-clients` request

1. **ICP sentence** — segment + trigger + buying role + disqualifier.
2. **Where to look** — the 2–3 best channels for this specific ICP, with what to pull from each.
3. **Qualifying filter** — the 3–4 signals that promote an entry from "name on a list" to "worth
   contacting".
4. **First-touch message** — ready to send, personalized on a placeholder detail, on the right
   channel, in the client's language.
5. **Cadence** — the multi-touch sequence and when to stop and re-cycle.
