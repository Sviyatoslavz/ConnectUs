# KPI, forecasting and sales planning

## Core metrics

| Metric | Formula | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| Conversion rate | stage N+1 / stage N | Where the funnel leaks |
| Win rate | won / (won + lost) | Quality of qualification |
| Average deal size | revenue / deals won | Segment and pricing health |
| Sales cycle | avg. days lead → payment | Velocity, cash flow |
| Sales velocity | (deals × avg. size × win rate) / cycle days | Revenue per day — the master metric |
| Pipeline coverage | pipeline value / target | Below 3× means the target is at risk |
| CAC | sales+marketing spend / new clients | Channel viability |
| LTV | avg. check × purchases per year × years | What you can afford to spend to acquire |
| LTV/CAC | — | Below 3 means the model is fragile |
| ROMI | (revenue − cost) / cost | Which channel to fund |
| Retention / churn | — | Whether growth is real or a treadmill |
| Follow-up speed | avg. minutes lead → first contact | Often the highest-leverage single fix |
| Meeting → opportunity | — | Quality of discovery |
| Opportunity → close | — | Quality of closing and pricing |

## Forecasting

Three methods; use at least two and compare.

1. **Weighted pipeline** — sum of (deal value × probability). Fast, but inflated if probabilities
   come from optimism instead of qualification criteria.
2. **Stage-based** — historical conversion rate of each stage applied to current volumes.
   More reliable when you have history.
3. **Velocity-based** — (opportunities × avg. size × win rate) / cycle length × forecast period.
   Best for detecting whether the current rate can hit the target at all.

Always produce three scenarios: pessimistic (only committed deals), realistic, optimistic
(everything with a scheduled next step). State the assumption behind each.

When data is incomplete, estimate from what exists and label the assumptions explicitly.
Refusing to forecast is worse than a clearly-labelled estimate.

## Gap analysis

Target − forecast = gap. Close it with the fastest available lever, in this order:

1. Deals already in late stages that need one push (fastest)
2. Existing clients — upsell, repeat, referral (cheapest)
3. Lost/dormant deals from the last 6 months (free to reactivate)
4. Increasing average check on deals in progress
5. New lead generation (slowest — usually can't affect the current period)

Reps consistently pick option 5 first. Redirect them.

## Sales plan structure

1. Revenue target and its breakdown by product, segment, rep
2. Required activity, worked backwards: deals needed → opportunities → meetings → contacts → leads
3. Daily/weekly activity norms per rep
4. Channels and expected volume from each
5. Team structure and territory split
6. Weekly control points — what gets reviewed, on which day
7. Motivation: fixed vs. variable, and what exactly the variable rewards

Always work backwards from the target through conversion rates. A plan that states revenue but
not the required weekly activity is a wish, not a plan.

## Dashboard (minimum viable)

Weekly: new leads, meetings held, proposals sent, deals won, revenue, pipeline value,
average time-in-stage, deals with no next step.

That last one — deals with no scheduled next step — predicts next month's revenue better than
almost anything else on the list.
