# Qualification and deal engineering

## MEDDPICC — use for any deal above a meaningful threshold or with more than one decision maker

| Element | What you need | Red flag |
|---|---|---|
| **M**etrics | Quantified impact the client stated themselves — money, hours, percentage | Numbers you invented, or "improved efficiency" |
| **E**conomic buyer | Named person with signature authority, met directly | "My manager will approve it" |
| **D**ecision criteria | The formal rules they will judge vendors by, in writing | You assume they'll judge on what you're good at |
| **D**ecision process | The actual steps and dates from evaluation to signature | "We'll get back to you" |
| **P**aper process | Legal, security, procurement, PO — and how long each takes | Discovered in the final week |
| **I**dentified pain | Root business problem, not a symptom, with cost attached | "They're interested" |
| **C**hampion | Has power, access, and personal upside from your success | A friendly contact who can't move anything |
| **C**ompetitors | Vendors, internal build, and doing nothing | Status quo not counted as a competitor |

The last one matters most: in enterprise deals, the winner is "no decision" more often than any rival vendor.

### Scorecard output
When asked to qualify a deal, produce a table: element | status (🔴/🟡/🟢) | evidence | how to fix it.
Then one line: the single weakest element and the exact action that closes the gap this week.
A deal with a red Economic Buyer or red Paper Process is not at the probability the rep thinks it is.

## Choosing a framework

| Framework | Focus | Best for | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| MEDDPICC | Full enterprise qualification | Long cycles, multiple stakeholders, large value | Overkill for short-cycle sales |
| BANT | Budget, Authority, Need, Timeline | Fast triage, transactional SMB | Ignores multi-stakeholder reality |
| ANUM | Authority first, then Need, Urgency, Money | Mid-market, fast pace | Can skip technical validation |
| FAINT | Funds, Authority, Interest, Need, Timing | Outbound where budget isn't allocated yet | Requires heavy value creation up front |
| CHAMP | Challenges before money | Inbound leads | Light on legal/paper process |

Pick one and apply it silently. Don't narrate the framework to the user unless they ask — they want
the answer, not the method.

## Probability discipline

Probability is derived from qualification evidence, not from mood. A useful rule:

- No confirmed pain → maximum 10%
- Pain confirmed, no economic buyer contact → maximum 25%
- Economic buyer engaged, criteria known → 50%
- Business case accepted, paper process mapped → 75%
- Signed → 100%

If a rep reports 80% on a deal where they've never met the person who signs, the forecast is fiction.
Say so plainly.
