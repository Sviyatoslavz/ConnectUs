# Funnel

## Standard stages

Lead → Contact → Company → Deal → Activities → Documents → Payment → Repeat sale → Referral

For B2B with a longer cycle, expand the middle:
Lead → Qualification → Discovery → Proposal → Negotiation → Contract → Payment → Onboarding → Expansion

For field sales / short-cycle SMB, compress it:
Territory list → First visit → Demo → Decision → Payment → Delivery → Repeat

## For any request, establish

- **Current stage** — where is this deal actually, not where the seller says it is
- **Stage goal** — the single outcome that moves it forward (not "build rapport")
- **Exit criterion** — the observable proof the stage is complete (meeting booked, spec confirmed,
  price approved by the person who signs)
- **Blocker** — what is physically preventing the exit criterion
- **Next action** — with exact wording if it's a message
- **Stage KPI** — conversion into the next stage, and time spent in this one

## Diagnosing drop-off

Find the stage where the biggest percentage is lost, and fix that one before touching anything
else. Typical patterns and their real causes:

| Symptom | Usual real cause |
|---|---|
| Many leads, few meetings | Wrong targeting, or first message is about you instead of them |
| Meetings but no proposals | Discovery too shallow — no confirmed pain, no budget conversation |
| Proposals but no answers | No decision maker in the room; proposal sent instead of presented |
| "We'll think about it" everywhere | No urgency created; the cost of doing nothing was never quantified |
| Deals close but don't repeat | No onboarding, no result measured, no reason to come back |
| Long cycle | Too many stages require the client to initiate; seller waits instead of scheduling |

## Rules that fix most funnels

- Never end a contact without the next contact scheduled at a specific date and time.
- Qualify out early. A fast "no" is worth more than a slow "maybe".
- The proposal is presented live, then sent — never sent cold and hoped for.
- Every stage has a named owner and a maximum allowed duration. Anything past that duration
  gets escalated or closed as lost.
- Measure time-in-stage, not just conversion. Velocity is where most revenue hides.

## Funnel audit checklist

1. How many leads enter per week, from which sources?
2. Conversion percentage between each pair of stages?
3. Average days in each stage?
4. What percentage of deals have a confirmed decision maker?
5. What percentage have a scheduled next step right now?
6. What percentage of losses are to a competitor vs. to "no decision"?
   (Losses to "no decision" mean the urgency work is broken, not the pricing.)
7. Repeat purchase rate and referral rate?
