# Negotiation

## Before the conversation

- **BATNA** — know yours and estimate theirs. Your leverage equals the quality of your alternative.
- **ZOPA** — the range where both sides can agree. If there isn't one, stop negotiating and requalify.
- **Walk-away point** — decided before the meeting, in writing, never during it.
- **Concession list** — everything you can give, ranked by what it costs you. Prepared in advance,
  never invented under pressure.

## Up-front contract (Sandler)

Agree before the conversation starts: how long, what you'll cover, what they want out of it, what
you want, and what outcomes are acceptable at the end — including "no". This single habit removes
most "мы подумаем" endings, because "think about it" was explicitly excluded as an outcome up front.

> "Предлагаю так: 30 минут. Я задам несколько вопросов по вашей ситуации, покажу, как это решается.
> В конце — либо договариваемся о следующем шаге, либо вы говорите, что не подходит, и мы спокойно
> расходимся. Оба варианта нормальные. Подходит?"

## Negative reverse selling

When the buyer is hesitant, pull back instead of pushing. Pressure creates resistance; withdrawal
creates re-engagement.

> "Судя по всему, сейчас это не в приоритете — может, нам действительно вернуться к этому позже?"

Use sparingly and never sarcastically. It works because it's a genuine offer to stop.

## The discount rule

Never concede price unilaterally. Every concession is traded for something of equal value:

| You give | You get |
|---|---|
| Price reduction | Longer term, prepayment, larger volume |
| Extended payment terms | Signature this month, or a larger scope |
| Extra scope or service | A case study, a reference call, or referrals |
| Faster delivery | Fewer customizations, or a standard configuration |

If a discount is granted for nothing, you've taught the buyer that your first price was dishonest —
and every future negotiation starts lower.

## Procurement

Procurement is measured on savings extracted, not on the deal's value. Expect: a late-stage discount
demand, a competing quote (sometimes real), and deliberate delay near your quarter end.

Counters: agree commercial terms with the economic buyer *before* procurement enters; keep one
concession in reserve for this exact moment; never let the quarter-end deadline be visible to them;
tie any discount to a signature date.

## Influence tools

Anchoring, contrast, social proof, scarcity when genuine, authority, loss aversion, reciprocity.
Use them to make a real value visible — not to manufacture urgency that doesn't exist. Fabricated
scarcity is discovered, and it costs the account.

## After the concession

Confirm in writing the same day: what was agreed, what was traded for it, and the next step with a
date. Verbal agreements decay within 48 hours.
