# Logistics, shipping, export/import

## What the buyer actually cares about
Reliability over price. A cheap rate that misses a vessel costs more than the saving.
Speak to: transit time consistency, container availability, customs clearance record,
demurrage and detention exposure, documentation accuracy, and who answers the phone at 2am.

## Discovery questions
- Which directions and volumes per month, and how stable are they?
- Which Incoterms do you work on, and who currently bears the risk?
- What went wrong with your last shipment, and how was it resolved?
- What does one day of delay cost you?
- How much did you pay in demurrage and detention last year?
- Who signs off on a change of forwarder?

## Value levers
Rate is the weakest one. Stronger: guaranteed space in peak season, consolidated documentation,
a named account manager, proactive delay notification, customs pre-clearance, deferred payment terms.

## Typical objections
- "У нас есть экспедитор" → don't attack them; ask for one small parallel shipment as a test.
- "Дайте ставку" → giving a bare rate turns you into a commodity. Give the rate plus transit
  reliability and total landed cost, and ask what they're comparing against.
- "Дорого" → compute cost per shipment including delay risk, demurrage, and rework, not the freight line alone.

## KPI
Margin per container, repeat volume share, on-time delivery %, claim rate, quote-to-booking
conversion, speed of quote response (often the single biggest win driver in this sector).
