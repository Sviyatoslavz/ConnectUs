# Local small business (cafés, salons, shops, service points)

Applies to field sales to owners of small local businesses — the highest-volume, shortest-cycle
sales motion, and the one where discipline matters more than technique.

## Who you're selling to
The owner is usually working inside the business, mid-task, and has been approached by three
salespeople this month. They decide fast, personally, and mostly on trust and visible proof.

## The first 15 seconds
Name their business specifically. Show, don't describe — a working demo in hand beats any pitch.
Then one question about their situation. Then a concrete next step with a date.

Never arrive at their peak hours. If they're busy, ask when to come back — and come back exactly
then. Doing that reliably puts you ahead of most competitors on its own.

## What they actually care about
More clients through the door, less time spent on admin, not looking outdated next to competitors,
and not being locked into something they can't cancel or maintain themselves.

They do not care about technology. Translate every feature into one of the four above.

## Objections
- "Дорого" → convert to cost per day and compare to one extra client per month.
- "Некогда" → emphasise that setup requires nothing from them beyond a photo and 5 minutes.
- "У меня уже есть Instagram/сайт" → position as complementary, not a replacement; focus on the
  gap it fills.
- "Подумаю" → the real reason is usually doubt that it'll work for *their* type of business.
  Show a case from a business on the same street or in the same category.

## Field sales discipline
- Territory divided by street or district, no overlap between reps.
- A visit is logged the same day — business name, contact, outcome, next step date.
- Every "no" gets a reason recorded from a fixed list. That list is what improves the pitch.
- Every "maybe" gets a scheduled return date, not a vague "I'll drop by".
- Reference clients are gold: one visible installed client per street makes the next ten sales easier.

## Pricing motion
One-off pricing is easy to sell and hard to build on. When moving to subscription, introduce it
with existing satisfied clients first, and price it against a measurable result they already saw.

## KPI
Visits per day, visits-to-demo, demo-to-sale, average check, repeat/subscription conversion,
referrals per client, revenue per territory per week, cost per acquired client per rep.
