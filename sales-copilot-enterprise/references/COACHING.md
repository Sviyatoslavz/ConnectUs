# Sales coaching

## Call evaluation — score 1 to 10

| Area | What a 9–10 looks like |
|---|---|
| Preparation | Knew the business before calling; opener referenced something specific |
| Opening | Earned attention in under 15 seconds, got explicit permission to continue |
| Listening | Talked under 40% of the time; didn't interrupt; used the client's own words back |
| Discovery | Reached consequence-level questions, not just situation questions |
| Qualification | Established budget, authority, timeline, decision process without interrogating |
| Presentation | Showed only what matched the stated pain; tied every feature to a result |
| Objection handling | Isolated the real objection, didn't get defensive, didn't discount reflexively |
| Closing | Asked directly, then stayed silent |
| Next step | Specific date and time agreed before hanging up |
| Discipline | Logged the deal, next step scheduled, promises kept |

Report the score per area, the weighted average, and — most usefully — the single lowest score
that is costing the most money right now. Coaching everything at once changes nothing.

## Feedback structure

1. What they did well, with a direct quote from what they said
2. The single most expensive mistake, with the cost made explicit
3. What to say instead — the exact replacement words
4. One drill to practice this week
5. How you'll both know it improved (a measurable behavior, not a feeling)

Do not deliver ten improvements at once. One change, practiced until automatic, beats ten noted
and forgotten.

## Common weaknesses and their drills

**Talks too much** — set a rule: no statement without a question after it. Record the next three
calls and count the talk ratio.

**Skips consequence questions** — before every call, write three "what does that cost you"
questions on paper. Not allowed to end discovery without asking at least two.

**Won't ask for the close** — practice ten direct closes in roleplay until the sentence stops
feeling rude.

**Discounts immediately** — ban unilateral discounting. Every concession must be traded for
something: term, prepayment, scope, referral.

**No scheduled next step** — hard rule: no call ends without a date in the calendar. Audit weekly;
this is the fastest measurable improvement available in most teams.

**Poor follow-up** — every follow-up must carry new information. Review their last five follow-ups
and rewrite them together.

## Onboarding a new rep (first 30 days)

- Week 1: product, ICP, pricing, objections learned cold. Shadow three real calls.
- Week 2: roleplay daily; first calls on lower-value leads with supervision.
- Week 3: independent calls, daily debrief on one recording.
- Week 4: own territory, weekly review against activity norms.

Measure activity in weeks 1–4, outcomes from month 2. Judging a new rep on closed revenue in
month one produces panic selling and discounting.

## Weekly team review — agenda

1. Activity against norms per rep — leads, meetings, proposals
2. Deals with no next step (fix them in the meeting, not after)
3. Top three deals: what specifically moves each one this week
4. Losses since last week and the reason for each
5. One skill topic — one, with a roleplay drill attached
